Quantum algorithms for electron microscopy

QuScope rebuilds transmission electron microscopy image formation as quantum circuits. The electron wavefunction is amplitude-encoded on qubits, and every optical element (specimen phase grating, Fresnel propagation, objective lens) is a diagonal unitary between quantum Fourier transforms, validated against classical references.

How it works

One circuit per image (CTEM) or per probe position (STEM). Slices repeat for thick specimens.

Validated pipelines

Noise-free simulations reproduce the classical reference implementations to unit fidelity.

CTEM (WPOA)

Bright-field imaging: phase grating, QFT, contrast transfer function, inverse QFT.

quantum_ctem_circuit

CTEM multislice

Alternating phase gratings and Fresnel propagators for thick specimens.

quantum_multislice_circuit

STEM (WPOA)

A focused probe per scan position with HAADF, ADF, ABF, BF and iDPC detectors.

STEM multislice

The probe state through the full multislice circuit at every scan position.

Toolkit

Electron optics

Relativistic wavelength and interaction constant, aberration function to 5th order (defocus, astigmatism, coma, spherical aberration, …), contrast transfer function, Kirkland scattering factors.

Quantum backends

Qiskit Aer statevector and shot-based simulation, noise models, and IBM Quantum hardware through a single backend manager.

Materials and validation

MoS2 and graphene workflows, classical multislice and WPOA references, resource estimates and circuit optimization.

Diffraction experimental

Quantum diffraction modes: SAED, CBED, nanobeam diffraction, Kikuchi and EBSD.

Thermal scattering experimental

Frozen phonons and thermal diffuse scattering: Debye–Waller channels, phonon superposition and Lindblad channels.

Bloch waves experimental

A Bloch-wave eigensolver based on quantum phase estimation.

Sister module

qptycho: quantum electron ptychography

qptycho is the QuScope project's ptychography module. It builds on QuScope's register, lens model, phase grating and backends to study the dose limits of electron ptychography for beam-sensitive materials and in-situ experiments.

  • STEM probe and multislice circuits validated against abTEM, including gas and liquid cells
  • Electron counting: one measurement shot is one detected electron
  • Quantum and classical Fisher information of the scattered electron, estimated with quantum circuits
  • Thermal vibrations and solvent configurations as an unmeasured configuration register
  • In-situ specimens: amorphous SiN and graphene windows, gas cells, liquid cells with window bulging

Repository in preparation (currently private). Physics fixes found while building it are in QuScope 0.2.1.

QuScope: An Open-Source Python Framework for Quantum-Circuit Simulation of Transmission Electron Microscopy

Sean D. Lam, Roberto dos Reis

The QuScope framework: conventional, multislice and scanning TEM pipelines as quantum circuits, validated against a classical reference, with resource estimates and an analysis of diagonal-gate synthesis on near-term hardware.

Quantum Algorithm Framework for Phase-Contrast Transmission Electron Microscopy Image Simulation

Sean D. Lam, Roberto dos Reis

Phase-contrast image formation on a gate-based quantum computer, validated on MoS2 against classical multislice, with a discussion of where quantum circuits may help: Fourier-space queries, global image statistics and phase-coherent observables.

Get started

# release
pip install quscope

# latest fixes (0.2.1)
git clone -b v0.2.x https://github.com/QuScope/QuScope
pip install -e QuScope
from quscope.quantum_ctem import QuantumCTEMCircuit, QuantumCTEMParameters
import numpy as np

params = QuantumCTEMParameters(acceleration_voltage=200e3, grid_size=8,
                               pixel_size=0.5, defocus=-659.7, cs=1.3)
result = QuantumCTEMCircuit(params).simulate(np.random.rand(8, 8) * 100)
print(result["intensity"].shape)   # (8, 8)
